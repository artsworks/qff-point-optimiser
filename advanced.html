How to use
  1. Enter your points balance and the number of travellers.
  2. Search the Flight Reward finder for Melbourne to Japan in Business and Economy. Its calendar covers 12 months. Note the dates with a seat for every traveller, and the taxes it shows.
  3. Add a journey for each date and route you want to compare. Enter the cash fares Qantas shows for the same flights. Each journey shows a verdict, and the ranking table puts the best journey first.

Gain is the value of the trip, minus the cash you pay, minus the points you use at your value per point. It is the dollar benefit over buying Economy and keeping your points.

Rules of thumb

  1. If Business Classic Reward seats exist for every traveller on dates you like, book them. Business is confirmed at booking.
  2. If there are no Business reward seats, check paid Economy with a points upgrade. Only fare classes G, K, L, M, S and V (78,500 points per person per flight in Zone 6) or B, H and Y (43,100) can be upgraded. Sale fares in classes such as E, N, O and Q can't.
  3. The upgrade route saves points compared with a Business reward, and costs more cash. The verdict shows what you pay in cash for each point you save.
  4. Qantas decides upgrades close to departure. A Business seat for sale doesn't mean an upgrade will clear. If it fails, you fly Economy and keep your points.
  5. If only one Business seat is free, search 1 to 3 days either side and other Japanese airports before you split up. Business one way and Economy the other is another choice.
  6. Reward seats open about 353 days before departure, and Qantas sometimes releases more closer to the date. Search the outbound and return flights separately.
Methodology, accuracy and sources
  • Points prices come from the Qantas tables for bookings made from 5 August 2025. Qantas prices each person and each one-way trip by zone, based on flown miles. This page estimates the miles from the route you enter, adding each leg. Qantas may count a route slightly differently, so the page warns you when a route is close to a zone limit. You can also set the zone yourself.
  • Partner flights, such as Japan Airlines, use a separate and more expensive table.
  • Cents per point is the cash value minus the cash you pay, divided by the points. For upgrades, the figure assumes the upgrade clears.
  • The value of an upgrade booking is P × the Business fare + (1 − P) × the Economy fare, where P is the chance it clears. The page counts the points as spent with chance P, because Qantas only takes them when the upgrade is confirmed.
  • The break-even chance is the upgrade chance at which the upgrade route and the Business reward have the same gain.
  • The page doesn't model upgrading an Economy Classic Reward. At 83,600 points per person per flight, the return trip would cost 239,600 points per person, more than a Business reward at 196,800.
  • Holiday and season dates cover 2027. They come from the Victorian Government calendar and approximate Japanese holidays and seasons. Check them before you book.
